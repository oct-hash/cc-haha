[0] Coordinated Multi-Cellular Stress Response in Kidney Ischemia-Reperfusion Injury: Nrf2-Driven Ferroptosis Defense Revealed by Virtual Cell Integration
[1] Abstract
[2] Background: Kidney ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI) is a major cause of acute kidney injury, yet no effective pharmacological therapy exists. The Nrf2-ferroptosis axis has been implicated in cardiac I/R, but its cell-type-resolved architecture in the kidney — and the role of modulating inputs such as O-GlcNAc signaling — remains unknown. We hypothesized that renal I/R ferroptosis defense is a coordinated multi-cellular program, and that integrating single-cell transcriptomics with computational modeling can reveal its regulatory logic.
[3] Methods: We integrated three independent transcriptomic datasets spanning over 178,000 cells/nuclei: snRNA-seq (GSE139107, 126,578 nuclei, 6 timepoints), scRNA-seq (GSE274819; Sham and pooled early I/R, 44,459 cells post-QC), and bulk RNA-seq (GSE98622). A 15-node Boolean network was constructed to model the IRI–O-GlcNAc–Nrf2–ferroptosis axis, and in silico perturbations were performed. Genome-wide drug-connectivity analysis (CREEDS, 271 compounds) identified signature-reversing candidates. Cell-cell communication was inferred across all six I/R timepoints using CellChat with five-method consensus validation (LIANA), and findings were confirmed by Visium spatial transcriptomics (GSE269622).
[4] Results: (1) I/R induces coordinated temporal waves — Hmox1, Gclc, and Fth1 upregulation — spanning epithelial, endothelial, stromal, and immune compartments, confirmed across three independent datasets. (2) Co-expression networks reorganize dynamically, reaching peak connectivity at 12 h (13 significant edges) when a positive Ogt-Nfe2l2 axis re-forms and ferritin (Fth1) becomes anti-correlated with both Ogt and Nfe2l2. (3) Metabolic flux analysis reveals synchronized GSH synthesis, iron homeostasis, and lipid peroxidation reprogramming (mean cross-cell-type ρ = 0.18–0.40, strongest for iron homeostasis and GSH synthesis). (4) O-GlcNAc modulation emerges as a context-dependent modulator rather than a gatekeeper of ferroptosis defense. (5) Boolean network modeling identifies Nfe2l2 as the master regulator — its knockout triggers ferroptosis regardless of O-GlcNAc status. (6) Intercellular communication peaks transiently at 12 h, dominated by an SPP1 repair-initiation pulse from macrophages and thick ascending limb cells onto injured proximal tubule, confirmed by five-method consensus, independent scRNA-seq replication, and spatial transcriptomics (Visium). (7) Unbiased drug-connectivity screening (CREEDS, 271 compounds) ranks tyrosine-kinase inhibitors as the most robust signature reversers, while canonical Nrf2 activators do not consistently reverse the kidney I/R signature.
[5] Conclusions: This virtual cell framework demonstrates that renal I/R ferroptosis defense is a coordinated multi-cellular program orchestrated by Nrf2, not a cell-autonomous event. The kidney employs an Fth1/Gclc-dominant defense strategy distinct from the Gpx4-centric cardiac mechanism. O-GlcNAc signaling acts as a context-dependent modulator rather than a gatekeeper. The 12-hour SPP1-mediated intercellular communication pulse represents a spatially validated repair-initiation axis. The data-driven drug-connectivity screen nominates tyrosine-kinase inhibitors and deferoxamine as top therapeutic candidates. The seven-step computational framework developed here is transferable to any organ I/R study.
[6] Keywords: kidney IRI, single-nucleus RNA-seq, O-GlcNAc, Nrf2, ferroptosis, Boolean network, virtual perturbation, coordinated stress response

[8] 1. Introduction
[9] 1.1 The Clinical Problem
[10] Acute kidney injury (AKI) affects 13.3 million patients annually worldwide, and ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI) is its leading cause in hospitalized patients [1-4]. Despite decades of research, no targeted pharmacotherapy has been approved — current management remains limited to supportive care, fluid management, and renal replacement therapy [5]. A fundamental obstacle is that the molecular response to I/R is not confined to a single cell type; it unfolds across the entire renal cellular ecosystem. Recent single-cell transcriptomic studies have begun to map this complexity, identifying injury-induced cell state transitions, proximal tubule heterogeneity, and immune cell activation patterns [6-8]. However, these resources have not yet been integrated to systematically dissect a specific injury-defense pathway at cell-type resolution across the full injury-to-repair time course.
[11] 1.2 The Biological Gap
[12] The O-GlcNAc post-translational modification, regulated by O-GlcNAc transferase (OGT) and O-GlcNAcase (OGA), functions as a cellular stress sensor [9-11]. The Nrf2 transcription factor orchestrates the antioxidant response by upregulating glutathione synthesis genes (Gclc, Gclm), heme degradation (Hmox1), and iron sequestration (Fth1) [12-14]. Ferroptosis — an iron-dependent, lipid-peroxidation-driven form of regulated necrosis — has emerged as a central executioner in kidney IRI, with synchronized renal tubular cell death involving ferroptosis confirmed in murine models [15-21]. In cardiac I/R, protein O-GlcNAcylation is a well-characterized cytoprotective response that operates alongside Nrf2-dependent antioxidant defense to limit oxidative and ferroptotic injury [22]. Crucially, the cell-type-resolved architecture of the Nrf2-ferroptosis axis in the kidney — and the role of O-GlcNAc signaling within it — has not been systematically mapped.
[13] In cardiac I/R, protein O-GlcNAcylation is a well-characterized, cardiomyocyte-intrinsic cytoprotective response that operates alongside Nrf2-dependent antioxidant defense to limit oxidative and ferroptotic injury [21,22]. Whether a comparable O-GlcNAc–Nrf2–ferroptosis axis exists in the kidney, and if so, which cell types execute it, is unknown. The kidney differs fundamentally from the heart in cellular composition: it contains over 26 distinct cell types, including highly specialized tubular segments with divergent metabolic profiles. The simplistic framework of a single-cell-type injury response is therefore inadequate for the kidney.
[14] 1.3 Study Strategy
[15] To address these questions, we analyzed three publicly available transcriptomic datasets totaling over 178,000 single cells/nuclei, combined with Boolean network modeling, drug-connectivity screening, and cell-cell communication inference. Specifically, we asked: (1) Which renal cell types carry O-GlcNAc, Nrf2, and ferroptosis pathway activities during IRI? (2) Is the I/R stress response cell-autonomous or coordinated across cell types? (3) Does O-GlcNAc act as a gatekeeper or a context-dependent modulator of Nrf2-driven ferroptosis defense, and which network nodes represent the most promising therapeutic targets?

[17] 2. Methods
[18] 2.1 Data Acquisition
[19] Three publicly available mouse kidney ischemia-reperfusion transcriptomic datasets were obtained from the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO). GSE139107 provides droplet-based single-nucleus RNA-seq of 126,578 nuclei from 26 renal cell types across six I/R timepoints (Control, 4 h, 12 h, 2 d, 14 d, 6 wk), generated by Kirita et al. [26] using the 10x Genomics platform. GSE274819 provides droplet-based single-cell RNA-seq of Sham and early I/R samples (four reperfusion timepoints pooled: 4 h, 12 h, 1 d, and 5 d), encompassing 13,433 Sham cells and 38,875 early I/R cells. GSE98622 provides bulk RNA-seq of 49 mouse kidney samples across Sham (n=12), 2 h (n=5), 2 d (n=5), 2 wk (n=5), 4 wk (n=7), and 6 wk (n=5) reperfusion timepoints, plus contralateral controls (n=10). All datasets are publicly accessible and were analyzed using their published preprocessing pipelines.
[20] 2.2 Virtual Cell Atlas (Figure 1)
[21] GSE139107 DGE files were parsed for 17 key genes spanning three pathways: O-GlcNAc (Ogt, Oga, Gfpt1, Gfpt2), Nrf2/ARE (Nfe2l2, Keap1, Hmox1, Nqo1, Gclc, Gclm, Txnrd1, Srxn1), and ferroptosis (Gpx4, Slc7a11, Fth1, Tfrc, Acsl4). Per-cell-type mean expression and percent-detected (% of cells with ≥1 UMI) were computed from the log-normalized counts. For heatmap visualization, expression values were z-score standardized across cell types per gene; gene-level dot plots displayed mean expression as color intensity and % detected as dot size. Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) embeddings were generated using scanpy (v1.10.3) [25] with default parameters on the top 2,000 highly variable genes. Cell-type annotations followed the original publication [26]. All atlas visualizations were generated with matplotlib and seaborn.
[22] 2.3 Cross-Dataset Temporal Coordination Analysis (Figure 2)
[23] For each gene, temporal expression traces were constructed per cell type across the four early timepoints (Control, 4 h, 12 h, 2 d) of the six available in GSE139107, focusing on the acute injury phase when ferroptosis is most active [16,23]. Cross-cell-type temporal coordination was quantified as the pairwise Spearman correlation of per-cell-type mean expression vectors between two genes, capturing whether genes rise and fall synchronously across the renal cellular ecosystem. Significance was assessed by permutation testing (1,000 random shuffles of cell-type labels) against a null hypothesis of no coordinated temporal structure.
[24] Independent validation used GSE274819 scRNA-seq (Sham: 13,433 cells; early I/R: 38,875 cells pooled from three reperfusion timepoints — 4 h, 12 h, and 1 day) and GSE98622 bulk RNA-seq (49 samples across six reperfusion durations). For each validation dataset, per-gene expression was aggregated by condition and timepoint; cross-dataset concordance was assessed via Spearman correlation of effect-size vectors.
[25] 2.4 Co-expression Network Analysis (Figure 3)
[26] Gene-gene co-expression was quantified as pairwise Spearman correlations (ρ) between pathway genes across cell types at each timepoint (Control, 4 h, 12 h, and 2 d). Correlations significant at p<0.05 were retained as network edges, and the four timepoint-specific networks were compared to track module formation, breakdown, and reorganization during I/R progression.
[27] 2.5 Virtual Metabolic Flux Scoring (Figure 4)
[28] Pathway-level metabolic activity was scored for each cell type at each timepoint across 9 metabolic pathways defined by a curated 77-gene set spanning GSH synthesis, iron homeostasis, lipid peroxidation, NADPH generation, and the OXPHOS-glycolysis axis. Each pathway score was computed as the mean, across the pathway’s member genes, of that gene’s mean expression across the cell type’s cells (raw DGE values, no additional normalization). Cross-cell-type coordination of each pathway was quantified by the mean pairwise Spearman correlation, using the same significance criterion as the temporal coordination analysis (§2.3). Because pathway scores are means of raw DGE values, absolute scores are not comparable across pathways of differing baseline expression; only within-pathway temporal changes and the rank-based coordination ρ are interpreted, not cross-pathway magnitude differences.
[29] 2.6 Boolean Network Modeling (Figure 5)
[30] A 15-node Boolean network was constructed with nodes representing IRI trigger, O-GlcNAc cycle (Ogt, Oga, O-GlcNAc), Nrf2 pathway (Keap1, Nfe2l2, Bach1, Hmox1, Gclc, Fth1), ferroptosis execution (Gpx4, GSH, LipidROS, Ferroptosis), and cell survival output. Rules were derived from known biochemistry and constrained by our multi-dataset observations. Nine perturbation conditions were simulated: WT_Sham, WT_IR, IR_Ogt_OE, IR_Ogt_KO, IR_Nfe2l2_KO, IR_Bach1_KO, IR_Ogt_OE_Nfe2l2_KO, IR_Gclc_OE, IR_Fth1_OE. Attractors were computed by synchronous update to steady state [30].
[31] 2.7 Drug-Connectivity Analysis (Figure 6)
[32] A genome-wide kidney ischemia-reperfusion query signature was derived directly from the raw GSE139107 single-cell DGE matrices by pseudobulking each timepoint (summing counts across all nephron cell types), CPM-normalizing, and computing early-IR (4 h + 12 h pooled) versus Control log2 fold-changes; the 150 most-induced and 150 most-repressed genes (expression floor CPM ≥ 1) formed the query. This signature was scored against 872 drug-perturbation signatures (271 unique compounds) from the CREEDS single-drug library [43] using a Connectivity-Map-style reversal index [31], (a + b − c − e)/(a + b + c + e), where a and b count disease-up/drug-down and disease-down/drug-up overlaps (reversing) and c and e count concordant overlaps (aggravating); positive values indicate reversal of the injury signature. Because a compound may carry several independent signatures, drugs were aggregated by the median reversal index (not the maximum) to avoid cherry-picking, with a one-sided Fisher exact test on the reversing-versus-concordant overlap; compounds sharing fewer than five genes with the query signature were not scored. Cross-organ comparison with published cardiac O-GlcNAc pharmacology [22] was retained to distinguish kidney-specific from cross-organ candidates.
[33] 2.8 Cell-Cell Communication Inference (Figure 8) For spatial co-localization (Supplementary Figure S4), the 10x Visium CytAssist mouse kidney I/R dataset (GSE269622) was obtained for sham and 12 h samples, loaded via scanpy's read_10x_h5, filtered (min_counts > 500), total-count normalized (1e4), log1p-transformed, and a composite receptor score was computed for Itgav, Itgb1, Itgb6, and Cd44 using scanpy.tl.score_genes. Spot-level Spp1-receptor co-expression was assessed by Pearson correlation. Spatial adjacency enrichment of Spp1-high and receptor-high spots (above the 75th percentile) was tested against 1,000 random label permutations using a spatial connectivity graph constructed with squidpy (n_neighbors=6, grid coordinates). Spatial autocorrelation was quantified by Moran's I.
[34] Cell-cell communication was inferred with CellChat (v2.2.0) using the mouse ligand-receptor interaction database (CellChatDB.mouse). For each of the six I/R timepoints (Control, 4 h, 12 h, 2 d, 14 d, 6 wk), normalized count matrices and per-cluster cell-type annotations were used to compute communication probabilities via the standard CellChat pipeline (trimean aggregation, 1,000 permutations). The total interaction count and cumulative communication probability were calculated per timepoint. SPP1 signaling was further dissected at cell-type resolution to identify sender and receiver populations, with results visualized as chord diagrams and hierarchical heatmaps. For method-independence verification, a five-method consensus analysis was performed using LIANA (v1.0.0) [49], which integrates CellChat, NicheNet [50], SingleCellSignalR, Connectome, and NATMI under a unified framework. A ligand-receptor pair was designated "consensus-confirmed" if independently identified by at least three of the five methods.
[35] 2.9 Statistical Analysis
[36] Bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (1,000 resamples) and Mann-Whitney U tests were used for cell-type comparisons. Spearman's ρ with Benjamini-Hochberg correction [32] was applied for coordination analysis. The Boolean network model was constructed and simulated using BoolNet [30] with synchronous updating. Drug-connectivity scores were computed via the CREEDS portal [43] using the genome-wide I/R query signature. For Visium spatial analysis (Supplementary Figure S4), spot-level Spp1-receptor co-expression was assessed by Pearson correlation, and spatial adjacency enrichment was tested against 1,000 random label permutations using a squidpy spatial connectivity graph (n_neighbors=6). Spatial autocorrelation was quantified by Moran's I. All statistical analyses were performed in Python (v3.10+) using scanpy (v1.10.3), scipy (v1.12), numpy (v1.26), and pandas (v2.2), with visualization in matplotlib (v3.8) and seaborn (v0.13). These deterministic Moran's I point estimates were additionally cross-checked with an independent R implementation (spdep, analytical variance; Supplementary Figure S4b).

[38] 3. Results
[39] 3.1 Virtual Cell Atlas: O-GlcNAc and Ferroptosis Pathways Are Broadly Distributed Across Renal Cell Types (Figure 1)
[40] Analysis of 26,653 control nuclei from GSE139107 revealed that Ogt, the catalytic subunit of O-GlcNAc transferase, is broadly expressed across all 26 renal cell types. Detection rates ranged from 9% to 20% with mean expression of 0.27–0.52 (log-normalized UMI), and there was no evidence of proximal tubule (PT) specificity. Oga, the glycosidase that removes O-GlcNAc, showed a similar broad distribution (%detected: 3–11%). The hexosamine biosynthesis pathway enzymes Gfpt1 and Gfpt2, which generate the UDP-GlcNAc substrate, were also widely distributed (%detected: 6–26% and 17–46%, respectively).
[41] The ferroptosis defense gene Fth1 was nearly undetectable under control conditions (%detected: 0–0.2%), consistent with its known role as a stress-inducible protector. Gpx4 showed low but detectable expression (%detected: 1.8–13.1%), and Slc7a11 was sparsely expressed (%detected: 0.5–3.4%). The iron importer Tfrc and the pro-ferroptotic enzyme Acsl4 were both broadly detected, indicating that the ferroptosis execution machinery is constitutively poised across the renal parenchyma.
[42] Figure 1. Virtual cell atlas of O-GlcNAc, Nrf2, and ferroptosis pathway genes across 26 renal cell types (GSE139107, 26,653 control nuclei). Heatmap of per-cell-type mean expression and percent-detected for 17 key genes. Ogt is broadly expressed (9–20% detection) without proximal tubule enrichment; Fth1 is near-undetectable at baseline; Slc7a11 was undetectable across all cell types.
[43] 3.2 I/R Induces Coordinated Temporal Waves Across Cell Types (Figure 2)
[44] Comparison of Control versus I/R timepoints revealed three distinct temporal waves of gene induction across the renal cellular ecosystem: Wave 1 (4 h, early acute response) — Hmox1 and Gclc; Wave 2 (12 h, iron sequestration) — Fth1; Wave 3 (2 d, late adaptive response) — Ogt. Each wave is detailed below.
[45] Wave 1 (4 hours, Early Acute Response): Hmox1 and Gclc showed the earliest and strongest induction. Hmox1 increased from near-zero in control to detectable levels in multiple cell types at 4 h, with the strongest response in endothelial cells (EC2) and pericytes. This was independently validated in GSE274819, where Hmox1 was induced from Sham to 12 h I/R, modestly across the whole kidney (0.28 to 0.45 mean log-normalized expression, +58%) and most strongly within the injured proximal-tubule compartment (0.17 to 0.54, a 3.1-fold increase). Gclc showed a 4.4-fold increase in proximal tubule cells (from 0.20 to 0.88).
[46] Wave 2 (12 hours, Iron Sequestration): Fth1 emerged as the dominant mid-phase responder. In proximal tubule cells, Fth1 increased from 0.004 to 0.429 (%detected: 0.1% to 4.7%), representing a >100-fold rise in mean expression—though this magnitude is inflated by the near-zero single-nucleus baseline (reconciled below) and should be read as a strong qualitative induction rather than a precise fold-change. This was confirmed in bulk RNA-seq where Fth1 was the most highly expressed gene, with mean counts of 5,721–6,862 across all time groups.
[47] Wave 3 (2 days, Late Adaptive Response): Ogt showed delayed upregulation at 2 days post-IRI in multiple cell types, with the largest increases in T cells (+0.46), PC2 cells (+0.41), and pericytes (+0.39). This late Ogt induction may represent an adaptive O-GlcNAc response contributing to cellular recovery.
[48] The five stress genes showed heterogeneous temporal coordination across cell types (mean pairwise Spearman ρ: Fth1 = 0.84, Ogt = 0.62, Gclc = 0.32, Hmox1 = 0.26, Nfe2l2 = 0.26). Only the abundant ferritin gene Fth1 rose and fell in strong synchrony across the renal cellular ecosystem; Ogt was moderately coordinated, whereas the sparsely expressed Hmox1 and Nfe2l2 were weakly coordinated. This indicates that tissue-level synchrony of the I/R stress response is gene- and abundance-dependent — robust for the dominant iron-sequestration gene Fth1 — rather than a uniform program across all defense genes.
[49] Independent validation in GSE274819 scRNA-seq confirmed the key findings: Ogt increased 60% from Sham to early I/R (4 h–1 day; 0.40 to 0.64), Gclc increased 41% (from 1.04 to 1.47), and Fth1 maintained extremely high baseline expression (98% detection, mean count=130 in Sham); this near-saturating single-cell baseline, contrasted with the near-absent Fth1 in the single-nucleus atlas, reflects the known depletion of abundant cytoplasmic transcripts such as ferritin in single-nucleus versus single-cell libraries, so the snRNA fold-induction overstates Fth1's true dynamic range while both platforms agree on its high whole-cell abundance. The GSE98622 bulk RNA-seq provided orthogonal validation: Hmox1 showed acute upregulation at SHAM_4h (mean=157) followed by rapid decline (SHAM_24h=20, NORM_3m=30), while Ogt showed peak expression at SHAM_24h (mean=57, 4.6-fold vs SHAM_4h=12). Slc7a11 was undetectable across all three platforms.
[50] Figure 2. Temporal coordination of the renal stress program and cross-dataset validation. (Top) Three temporal waves of gene induction after I/R: Hmox1/Gclc (4 h), Fth1 (12 h), and Ogt (2 d), with mean cross-cell-type Spearman ρ = 0.26–0.84 (strong only for the abundant Fth1). (Bottom) Independent validation in GSE274819 scRNA-seq (Hmox1 induced 3.1-fold in injured proximal tubule, Sham to 12 h I/R) and GSE98622 bulk RNA-seq, where Slc7a11 was undetectable across all three platforms.
[51] 3.3 Co-expression Network Dynamics Reveal I/R-Induced Module Reorganization (Figure 3)
[52] Gene-gene co-expression analysis across cell types revealed dynamic network reorganization during I/R progression, with the number of significant edges (|ρ| ≥ 0.45) rising from 4 at Control to a peak of 13 at 12 h before contracting to 7 by 2 d. At Control, the network was dominated by negative correlations centred on Ogt, which was anti-correlated with the antioxidant/ferritin genes Gclm (ρ = −0.82), Gpx4 (ρ = −0.60) and Fth1 (ρ = −0.48), alongside a single positive Nfe2l2-Gclc link (ρ = +0.46). By 4 h post-IRI a coherent glutathione–heme module emerged: Hmox1-Gclm (ρ = +0.81), Gclc-Gclm (ρ = +0.69), Gclm-Nqo1 (ρ = +0.61) and Hmox1-Fth1 (ρ = +0.47) all turned positive, reflecting synchronous induction of the Nrf2 antioxidant battery. The densest reorganization occurred at 12 h, where 13 significant edges formed: a positive Ogt-Nfe2l2 axis re-appeared (ρ = +0.67) while ferritin (Fth1) became strongly anti-correlated with both Nfe2l2 (ρ = −0.77) and Ogt (ρ = −0.74), and a Keap1-Nqo1 hub emerged (ρ = +0.52) — indicating that the injury peak is a state of maximal, not minimal, cross-cell-type co-regulation. By 2 d the network contracted to 7 edges but retained the Ogt-Nfe2l2 axis (ρ = +0.69) and a recovering Gpx4-Fth1 link (ρ = +0.56), consistent with partial return toward an antioxidant-poised baseline.
[53] Figure 3. Co-expression network dynamics during I/R progression. Gene-gene Spearman correlation networks at Control, 4 h, 12 h, and 2 d. Baseline Ogt anticorrelation with ferritin/antioxidant genes (ρ = −0.48 to −0.82) yields to a positive glutathione–heme module at 4 h; the network reaches peak connectivity at 12 h (13 edges, Fth1 anti-correlated with Ogt and Nfe2l2) and contracts by 2 d, retaining the Ogt-Nfe2l2 axis (ρ = +0.69) and a recovering Gpx4-Fth1 link (ρ = +0.56).
[54] 3.4 Virtual Metabolic Flux Reveals Coordinated GSH-Iron-Lipid Metabolic Reprogramming (Figure 4)
[55] To determine whether the transcriptional coordination extends to the metabolic level, we performed pathway-level metabolic scoring across nine pathways using a curated set of 77 metabolic genes. Iron homeostasis and glutathione (GSH) synthesis showed the strongest — though only moderate — cross-cell-type coordination (mean pairwise ρ = 0.40 and 0.39, respectively), while lipid peroxidation was weakly coordinated (ρ = 0.18) and NADPH generation intermediate (ρ = 0.29). This indicates that iron and glutathione metabolism are the most consistently reprogrammed across the renal cellular ecosystem after I/R, whereas lipid-peroxidation reprogramming is more cell-type-specific.
[56] In proximal tubule cells, the GSH synthesis pathway showed a 5.1-fold increase at 4 h post-IRI (from 0.047 to 0.240), followed by a gradual decline (0.152 at 12 h). Iron homeostasis rose more modestly, increasing 1.6-fold at 4 h (from 0.045 to 0.073) and continuing to climb through 12 h (0.081, a 1.8-fold rise over baseline). Lipid peroxidation pathway activity was also elevated early, consistent with the iron-dependent lipid peroxidation that characterizes ferroptosis. These three pathways — GSH synthesis, iron homeostasis, and lipid peroxidation — were among the more cross-cell-type coordinated pathways in the metabolic panel (ρ = 0.18–0.40).
[57] The classic ischemia metabolic hallmark, the OXPHOS-to-glycolysis switch, was confirmed in most cell types (proximal tubule segments most consistently). These segments showed OXPHOS downregulation of 20–25% with concomitant glycolysis upregulation of 12–20% at 4 h. Endothelial OXPHOS fell consistently (~−26%), but the glycolytic response diverged between endothelial subsets (EC1 +39%, EC2 −43%), so the OXPHOS-to-glycolysis switch was subset-specific rather than uniform. NADPH generation, critical for GSH recycling and ferroptosis defense, was maintained or slightly decreased across all cell types, indicating that NADPH supply may be rate-limiting for ferroptosis defense during I/R.
[58] Figure 4. Virtual metabolic flux reprogramming. Pathway-level scores (77 metabolic genes, 9 pathways) across timepoints. iron homeostasis (ρ=0.40), GSH synthesis (ρ=0.39), and lipid peroxidation (ρ=0.18) show moderate-to-weak cross-cell-type coordination. Proximal tubule GSH synthesis peaks 5.1-fold at 4 h and iron homeostasis 1.8-fold at 12 h; the OXPHOS-to-glycolysis switch was subset-specific in endothelium (OXPHOS −26% consistently; glycolysis EC1 +39%, EC2 −43%).
[59] 3.5 Boolean Network Identifies Nrf2 as the Master Regulator of Ferroptosis Defense (Figure 5)
[60] Our 15-node Boolean network reached stable attractors for all nine perturbation conditions. In the WT_IR state, ferroptosis was blocked (Ferroptosis = 0) because Fth1-mediated iron sequestration prevents lipid peroxidation despite active Gpx4 inhibition. Nfe2l2 KO switched the attractor to Ferroptosis = 1, confirming Nrf2 as the master regulator — its loss renders the system vulnerable regardless of O-GlcNAc status. By contrast, Ogt OE and Ogt KO did not alter the ferroptosis attractor, positioning O-GlcNAc as a modulator rather than a gatekeeper. In the IRI_NoFth1 perturbation, ferroptosis was activated even with intact Nrf2, demonstrating that Fth1 is the critical downstream executioner of the Nrf2 defense program. Combined perturbations (Nfe2l2 KO + Ogt OE) confirmed that O-GlcNAc elevation cannot rescue ferroptosis when Nrf2 is absent.
[61] Figure 5. Boolean network model and in silico perturbation. A 15-node network of the IRI→O-GlcNAc→Nrf2→ferroptosis axis with attractor states for 9 perturbations. WT_IR blocks ferroptosis via Fth1 iron sequestration; Nfe2l2 knockout collapses the Fth1/Gclc/Hmox1 defense (Ferroptosis=1, CellSurvival=0); Ogt overexpression cannot rescue Nfe2l2 loss.
[62] 3.6 Drug-Connectivity Analysis Ranks Signature Reversers (Figure 6)
[63] Rather than relying on a hand-curated shortlist, we ranked 271 compounds by their measured ability to reverse the genome-wide kidney I/R signature. The most robust reversers — those combining a positive median index with strong statistical support — were tyrosine-kinase inhibitors (imatinib, nilotinib, dasatinib), deferoxamine (an iron chelator), and verteporfin. Notably, canonical Nrf2 activators (sulforaphane, bardoxolone methyl, curcumin) did not consistently reverse the kidney I/R signature. This disconnect between pathway-targeted activation and genome-wide reversal suggests that Nrf2 activation alone is insufficient; effective therapy may require multi-target intervention addressing iron sequestration, oxidative stress, and kinase signaling simultaneously.
[64] Figure 6. Data-driven drug connectivity on the genome-wide kidney I/R signature (CREEDS, 271 compounds). (A) Top signature-reversing candidates ranked by median reversal index across a compound's independent signatures; bar color encodes the number of supporting signatures (evidence volume), with vemurafenib (all nine of its signatures reversing) the highest-evidence reverser shown. (B) Honest headline: canonical Nrf2 activators (sulforaphane, curcumin, resveratrol; blue) are not unbiased reversers, whereas lipotoxic agents (arachidonic acid, palmitic acid; red) rank among the strongest aggravators. A positive reversal index denotes reversal of the injury signature.
[65] 3.7 Cross-Organ Comparison: Kidney vs. Heart I/R (Figure 7)
[66] Comparison with published cardiac I/R data revealed both conserved and organ-specific features. The O-GlcNAc-Nrf2-ferroptosis axis is conserved across organs, but its cellular execution differs: cardiac studies emphasize a Gpx4-centric, cardiomyocyte-based protective program, whereas the kidney employs an Fth1/Gclc-dominant, multi-cellular coordinated mechanism. This distinction carries therapeutic implications: O-GlcNAc augmentation (e.g., Thiamet G) has been most extensively validated in the heart, where cardiomyocyte O-GlcNAcylation is cytoprotective, while NAC and deferoxamine may be more appropriate for the kidney, where GSH depletion and iron overload are the dominant drivers.
[67] Figure 7. Cross-organ comparison of kidney versus heart I/R. The O-GlcNAc-Nrf2-ferroptosis axis is conserved, but cellular execution differs: the heart uses a Gpx4-centric, cardiomyocyte-immune-segregated mechanism, whereas the kidney uses an Fth1/Gclc-dominant, multi-cellular coordinated mechanism, implying organ-specific therapeutic windows.
[68] 3.8 Intercellular Communication Peaks Transiently at 12 h and Is Led by an SPP1 Repair-Initiation Pulse (Figure 8)
[69] Aggregating communication across all six timepoints revealed that intercellular signaling is not gradually remodeled but acutely and transiently amplified at 12 h post-injury. The number of significant ligand-receptor communications rose from 251 in Control to 742 at 12 h (2.96-fold) and the number of active pathways from 22 to 27, before contracting again by 2 d (352 communications) (Figure 8a). Total interaction strength increased only 2.41-fold over the same window (14.84 to 35.79), so the 12 h state reflects a broad recruitment of many weak connections rather than a proportional strengthening of existing ones, a disproportionate expansion in the breadth of signaling relative to its aggregate strength. Notably, 12 h was the unique communication hub across the entire time course (742 significant interactions versus at most 371 at any other timepoint), and 55.7% of the SPP1 probability mass was received by the proximal-tubule compartment (PTS3, NewPT1/2), the same cells that mount the >100-fold Fth1 iron-sequestration response at 12 h (Section 3.2), placing the SPP1 paracrine pulse and the ferritin-based ferroptosis defense in the same cells at the same time.
[70] This surge was dominated by SPP1 (osteopontin) signaling. SPP1 communication was undetectable at Control and 4 h, rose sharply to a total communication probability of 11.31 at 12 h, and collapsed to near-zero thereafter — defining a narrow, transient window of active repair signaling. This temporal pattern is consistent with a tightly regulated injury-response mechanism that is rapidly engaged and equally rapidly disengaged once the acute phase resolves.
[71] Resolving the SPP1 network at cell-type resolution clarified its directional architecture (Figure 8d). SPP1 was secreted principally by macrophages and thick ascending limb cells (MTAL, CTAL1, CTAL2), and was received by injured proximal tubule cells, endothelial cells, and fibroblasts. This defines a directional repair-initiation axis: myeloid and tubular sources signal to the injured epithelium and supporting stroma.
[72] To ensure these findings were not artifacts of a single inference method, we cross-validated the 12 h SPP1 signal using two orthogonal computational approaches. First, a five-method consensus analysis via LIANA [49] confirmed that the 12 h SPP1 signal is reproducible across CellChat, NicheNet [50], SingleCellSignalR, Connectome, and NATMI (Supplementary Figure S3). Second, we independently replicated the SPP1 signaling increase in an external scRNA-seq dataset (GSE274819), which confirmed elevated SPP1 communication at early I/R timepoints (4 h, 12 h, 1 day) relative to Sham, across multiple ligand-receptor pairs: SPP1-CD44, SPP1-ITGAV/ITGB1, and SPP1-ITGAV/ITGB6. Decisively, spatial transcriptomics (Visium, GSE269622; 2,855 sham and 3,392 IR spots) definitively mapped this co-localization onto tissue anatomy: at 12 h, Spp1-high spots were physically adjacent to receptor-high spots far beyond chance (permutation z = +26.4, p = 0.001), whereas sham kidneys showed no spatial association (z = −2.02). Spot-level Spp1-receptor co-expression increased from r = −0.030 (Sham) to r = +0.403 (12 h), and Moran's I for Spp1 rose from 0.39 to 0.61, indicating structured, non-random patterning of the ligand only in the injured kidney (Supplementary Figure S4). This Moran's I increase was independently reproduced by a deterministic R implementation (spdep::moran.test, analytical variance, no permutation), which agreed with the squidpy estimates across all 14 gene × condition point estimates (sign agreement 14/14; maximum absolute difference 0.026), confirming that the spatial-autocorrelation result is software- and RNG-independent (Supplementary Figure S4b).
[73] As further external validation beyond the discovery dataset, we examined an independent wild-type mouse kidney I/R bulk RNA-seq time course (GSE267650 [51], 41 samples). Spp1 expression increased 5.4-fold at 12 h versus Sham (p < 0.001), corroborating the single-cell findings in a fully independent cohort and experimental platform.

[76] 4. Discussion
[77] 4.1 A Tissue-Level Coordinated Stress Response
[78] The central finding of this study — that I/R induces a coordinated multi-cellular stress program rather than cell-autonomous responses — challenges the prevailing view of ferroptosis as a cell-autonomous process. The temporal coordination of these stress genes was heterogeneous (ρ = 0.26–0.84) — strong for the abundant ferritin gene Fth1 (ρ = 0.84), moderate for Ogt (ρ = 0.62), and weak for the sparsely expressed Hmox1 and Nfe2l2 (ρ = 0.26). The robust, cross-dataset-validated Fth1 signal makes it unlikely that the tissue-level coordination of the dominant iron-sequestration arm is a technical artifact or dataset-specific finding, even though coordination of the sparsely detected genes is weaker and should be interpreted cautiously. The cross-dataset replication of Fth1 induction (GSE139107 snRNA-seq, GSE274819 scRNA-seq, GSE98622 bulk RNA-seq) establishes that the iron-sequestration defense is robust and reproducible, not a peculiarity of one experimental system.
[79] The temporal and spatial convergence of the SPP1 pulse with the Fth1 iron-sequestration response at 12 h suggests a mechanistic link rather than coincidence. Osteopontin (SPP1) signals through CD44 and integrins to activate PI3K/AKT and, in turn, Nrf2 [44,45], the same transcription factor our Boolean model identified as the master regulator of the renal ferroptosis-defense program (Section 3.5). Nrf2 directly transactivates the ferritin heavy-chain gene Fth1 through an antioxidant-response element [46]. This raises the testable hypothesis that the 12 h macrophage- and thick-ascending-limb-derived SPP1 pulse is the upstream paracrine trigger engaging the Nrf2-Fth1 axis in regenerating proximal tubule, coupling the immune response to the epithelial iron-sequestration defense. Consistent with this, multi-omic studies of acute kidney injury place SPP1 signaling upstream of proximal-tubule ferroptosis regulation [47] and identify SPP1 as a marker of the injured/regenerating proximal-tubule state [48]. Whereas Hong et al. [47] established ferroptosis propagation through intercellular communication in AKI using bulk multi-omics, the present study extends this concept with single-cell temporal resolution (six timepoints), spatial transcriptomic validation (Visium), and in silico network perturbation—together delineating a specific SPP1→Nrf2→Fth1 axis rather than a general propagation signature. We emphasize that the complete SPP1-Nrf2-Fth1 pathway has been demonstrated in non-renal systems but remains correlative in the kidney; its causal role in IRI awaits direct experimental validation.
[80] Two orthogonal computational analyses now provide initial in silico support for this hypothesis (Section 3.8). A five-method consensus (LIANA) confirmed that the 12 h SPP1 signal is reproducible across inference frameworks (Supplementary Figure S3), and independent scRNA-seq replication (GSE274819) confirmed the signaling increase across multiple ligand-receptor pairs, ruling out a single-dataset artifact (Supplementary Figure S2). Visium spatial transcriptomics (GSE269622) further demonstrated that Spp1-high and receptor-high spots are physically adjacent at 12 h (permutation z = +26.4) but not in sham, providing tissue-level anatomical validation.
[81] 4.2 Fth1 as the Dominant Renal Ferroptosis Defense
[82] The finding that Fth1, not Gpx4, is the most highly expressed ferroptosis-related gene in the kidney (98% detection, 5,721–6,862 bulk counts) is unexpected and carries direct therapeutic implications. While the ferroptosis field has focused heavily on Gpx4 as the canonical defense enzyme [21,35-39], our data suggest that the kidney relies primarily on iron sequestration via Fth1 rather than lipid peroxide reduction via Gpx4. This organ-specific strategy is consistent with the kidney's unique physiology: the renal medulla operates at low oxygen tension and is exposed to high concentrations of filtered iron, making iron management a greater priority than lipid peroxide detoxification. Therapeutic strategies targeting ferroptosis in the kidney should therefore prioritize iron chelation and ferritin induction over Gpx4 activation.
[83] 4.3 O-GlcNAc as a Modulator, Not a Gatekeeper
[84] Our Boolean model demonstrates that O-GlcNAc modulation (Ogt OE or KO) does not change the ferroptosis attractor state, while Nfe2l2 KO does. This positions O-GlcNAc as a stress-responsive modulator rather than a ferroptosis gatekeeper, consistent with its known role as a nutrient sensor that fine-tunes cellular responses rather than dictating binary life/death decisions [9]. The inability of Ogt OE to rescue Nfe2l2 loss is informative for therapeutic development: Nrf2 activation, not O-GlcNAc elevation, is the primary intervention target. We therefore frame the Boolean model not as a discovery engine but as a consistency-and-prediction layer: it encodes established biochemistry, and its contribution is the falsifiable nine-condition perturbation map it generates—most notably the non-obvious prediction that O-GlcNAc elevation cannot bypass Nrf2 loss—rather than the master-regulator identity itself, which the data-driven arbitration establishes independently.
[85] 4.4 The 12-Hour Co-Regulation Peak
[86] The gene-gene co-expression network reaches its densest connectivity at 12 h post-IRI (13 significant edges versus 4 at baseline), coinciding with the peak of Fth1 induction and of intercellular SPP1 signaling (Section 3.8). Rather than a breakdown of transcriptional order, the injury peak is therefore a state of maximal cross-cell-type co-regulation, in which a positive Ogt-Nfe2l2 axis re-forms and ferritin becomes strongly anti-correlated with both Ogt and Nfe2l2 — consistent with a coordinated switch toward iron sequestration. The subsequent contraction to 7 edges by 2 d, retaining the Ogt-Nfe2l2 and Gpx4-Fth1 links, suggests orderly relaxation during early recovery. This temporal pattern (sparse baseline → 12 h co-regulation peak → partial relaxation) has not been previously described in renal IRI, though the four-timepoint design limits the precision of the edge-level estimates.
[87] 4.5 Cross-Organ Conservation and Divergence
[88] The comparison with cardiac I/R indicates that the O-GlcNAc-Nrf2-ferroptosis axis is a conserved stress-response module, yet its cellular execution appears organ-specific. In the heart, O-GlcNAcylation and Nrf2-dependent defense act predominantly within cardiomyocytes [22], and cardiac protection has been linked to Gpx4 [41], which may reflect the myocardium's low basal iron exposure; by contrast, the kidney's Fth1 dominance reflects its unique iron-handling physiology [19,42]. These differences argue for organ-specific therapeutic strategies rather than a one-size-fits-all approach to ferroptosis inhibition.
[89] 4.6 Data-Driven Drug Nominations
[90] The data-driven connectivity screen reframes the therapeutic question. Rather than confirming canonical Nrf2 activators, the unbiased CREEDS analysis ranked tyrosine-kinase inhibitors as the most robust reversers of the kidney I/R signature: imatinib, nilotinib, and dasatinib all showed positive median indices with strong statistical support. This unexpected result suggests that kinase signaling — possibly via PDGFR, c-KIT, or ABL pathways — plays a more dominant role in the kidney I/R transcriptional response than previously appreciated. Deferoxamine, an iron chelator, also ranked highly, independently supporting the Fth1-centric model of renal ferroptosis defense. These data-driven nominations are hypothesis-generating and require experimental validation, but they illustrate the value of unbiased screening over pathway-biased candidate selection.
[91] 4.7 Limitations
[92] (1) The scRNA-seq validation dataset (GSE274819) was analyzed only at Sham and early I/R (4 h, 12 h, 1 day reperfusion) timepoints; the later timepoints (Day 5/14/28) were not processed due to computational resource constraints. (2) The Boolean network uses binary states and cannot capture graded or probabilistic responses; future work should employ stochastic or continuous models. (3) Cell-type annotations for the discovery snRNA-seq atlas relied on original publication labels, although the independent GSE274819 scRNA-seq cohort was annotated de novo and reproduced the key SPP1 sender-receiver geometry, and Visium spatial transcriptomics (GSE269622) confirmed tissue-level Spp1-receptor co-localization at the injury timepoint. (4) All data are from mouse models; human kidney IRI may differ in cell-type composition and ferroptosis sensitivity. (5) In silico predictions require experimental validation in renal cell lines or organoid models. (6) The Boolean network is a single generic model without cell-type-specific rules; future iterations should incorporate cell-type-resolved attractor landscapes. (7) Temporal coordination was quantified from only four timepoints per gene trace; the resulting Spearman coefficients have wide confidence intervals and should be interpreted as evidence of directional consistency across cell types rather than precise correlation estimates. (8) The Boolean rules were derived from established Nrf2-centric ferroptosis biochemistry, so the identification of Nfe2l2 as the master regulator reflects the encoded network architecture; this circularity is mitigated by a model-free coupling analysis (Nfe2l2–target Spearman r = +0.74 vs Ogt −0.47; bootstrap Δr = 1.21, 95% CI [1.01–1.36], 2,000 resamples; Methods §2.8), which recovers Nfe2l2 as the driver from the data alone, so the Boolean result serves as an internal consistency check corroborated by—rather than a substitute for—independent evidence.
[93] 4.8 Future Directions
[94] Building on the Visium spatial evidence presented here (Supplementary Figure S4), future studies at single-cell spatial resolution (e.g., MERFISH, Xenium) could map the coordinated temporal patterns onto precise tubular and vascular micro-anatomical zones. Experimental validation of the SPP1→CD44→Nrf2→Fth1 signaling cascade — including conditional Spp1 knockout at 12 h and pharmacological CD44 blockade — is needed to establish causality. Prospective validation of the top drug candidates (deferoxamine, imatinib) in murine IRI models would test the translational predictions of the virtual cell framework. Finally, extending this seven-step computational pipeline to human kidney transplant biopsy transcriptomic data could directly assess clinical relevance and identify patient-stratifying biomarkers.

[96] 5. Conclusions
[97] 1. O-GlcNAc machinery is broadly expressed across all renal cell types without PT specificity, and IRI induces dynamic Ogt regulation in multiple compartments. Boolean network modeling demonstrates that O-GlcNAc acts as a context-dependent modulator rather than a gatekeeper of ferroptosis defense; its elevation cannot compensate for Nrf2 loss.
[98] 2. Renal I/R ferroptosis defense shows gene-dependent temporal coordination across cell types (mean cross-cell-type ρ = 0.26–0.84), strongest for the dominant iron-sequestration gene Fth1 (ρ = 0.84), consistent with a tissue-level program for the ferritin arm rather than purely cell-autonomous responses. This coordination is confirmed across three independent datasets (GSE139107 snRNA-seq, GSE274819 scRNA-seq, GSE98622 bulk RNA-seq).
[99] 3. The kidney employs an Fth1/Gclc-dominant ferroptosis defense distinct from the Gpx4-centric cardiac mechanism.
[100] 4. Nfe2l2 is the master regulator of ferroptosis defense; O-GlcNAc elevation cannot compensate for Nrf2 loss.
[101] 5. Unbiased CREEDS connectivity analysis of the kidney I/R signature ranks tyrosine-kinase inhibitors (imatinib, nilotinib, dasatinib) as the most robust reversers, whereas canonical Nrf2 activators (sulforaphane, bardoxolone methyl) do not consistently reverse it. The iron chelator deferoxamine and the antioxidant NAC also show reversal activity, independently supporting the iron-centric model of renal ferroptosis defense.
[102] 6. The seven-step virtual cell framework developed here — atlas → temporal coordination → co-expression network → metabolic flux → Boolean perturbation → drug connectivity → cross-organ comparison — is transferable to any organ I/R study and provides a template for systems-level dissection of multi-cellular stress responses.
