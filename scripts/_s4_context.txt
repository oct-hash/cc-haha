Methods: We integrated three independent transcriptomic datasets spanning over 178,000 cells/nuclei: snRNA-seq (GSE139107, 126,578 nuclei, 6 timepoints), scRNA-seq (GSE274819; Sham and pooled early I/R, 44,459 cells post-QC), and bulk RNA-seq (GSE98622). A 15-node Boolean network was constructed to model the IRI–O-GlcNAc–Nrf2–ferroptosis axis, and in silico perturbations were performed. Genome-wide drug-connectivity analysis (CREEDS, 271 compounds) identified signature-reversing candidates. Cell-cell communication was inferred across all six I/R timepoints using CellChat with five-method consensus validation (LIANA), and findings were confirmed by Visium spatial transcriptomics (GSE269622).

---- [4] (style=Normal) ----
Results: (1) I/R induces coordinated temporal waves — Hmox1, Gclc, and Fth1 upregulation — spanning epithelial, endothelial, stromal, and immune compartments, confirmed across three independent datasets. (2) Co-expression networks reorganize dynamically, reaching peak connectivity at 12 h (13 significant edges) when a positive Ogt-Nfe2l2 axis re-forms and ferritin (Fth1) becomes anti-correlated with both Ogt and Nfe2l2. (3) Metabolic flux analysis reveals synchronized GSH synthesis, iron homeostasis, and lipid peroxidation reprogramming (mean cross-cell-type ρ = 0.18–0.40, strongest for iron homeostasis and GSH synthesis). (4) O-GlcNAc modulation emerges as a context-dependent modulator rather than a gatekeeper of ferroptosis defense. (5) Boolean network modeling identifies Nfe2l2 as the master regulator — its knockout triggers ferroptosis regardless of O-GlcNAc status. (6) Intercellular communication peaks transiently at 12 h, dominated by an SPP1 repair-initiation pulse from macrophages and thick ascending limb cells onto injured proximal tubule, confirmed by five-method consensus, independent scRNA-seq replication, and spatial transcriptomics (Visium). (7) Unbiased drug-connectivity screening (CREEDS, 271 compounds) ranks tyrosine-kinase inhibitors as the most robust signature reversers, while canonical Nrf2 activators do not consistently reverse the kidney I/R signature.

2.8 Cell-Cell Communication Inference (Figure 8) For spatial co-localization (Supplementary Figure S4), the 10x Visium CytAssist mouse kidney I/R dataset (GSE269622) was obtained for sham and 12 h samples, loaded via scanpy's read_10x_h5, filtered (min_counts > 500), total-count normalized (1e4), log1p-transformed, and a composite receptor score was computed for Itgav, Itgb1, Itgb6, and Cd44 using scanpy.tl.score_genes. Spot-level Spp1-receptor co-expression was assessed by Pearson correlation. Spatial adjacency enrichment of Spp1-high and receptor-high spots (above the 75th percentile) was tested against 1,000 random label permutations using a spatial connectivity graph constructed with squidpy (n_neighbors=6, grid coordinates). Spatial autocorrelation was quantified by Moran's I.

Bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (1,000 resamples) and Mann-Whitney U tests were used for cell-type comparisons. Spearman's ρ with Benjamini-Hochberg correction [32] was applied for coordination analysis. The Boolean network model was constructed and simulated using BoolNet [30] with synchronous updating. Drug-connectivity scores were computed via the CREEDS portal [43] using the genome-wide I/R query signature. For Visium spatial analysis (Supplementary Figure S4), spot-level Spp1-receptor co-expression was assessed by Pearson correlation, and spatial adjacency enrichment was tested against 1,000 random label permutations using a squidpy spatial connectivity graph (n_neighbors=6). Spatial autocorrelation was quantified by Moran's I. All statistical analyses were performed in Python (v3.10+) using scanpy (v1.10.3), scipy (v1.12), numpy (v1.26), and pandas (v2.2), with visualization in matplotlib (v3.8) and seaborn (v0.13).

To ensure these findings were not artifacts of a single inference method, we cross-validated the 12 h SPP1 signal using two orthogonal computational approaches. First, a five-method consensus analysis via LIANA [49] confirmed that the 12 h SPP1 signal is reproducible across CellChat, NicheNet [50], SingleCellSignalR, Connectome, and NATMI (Supplementary Figure S3). Second, we independently replicated the SPP1 signaling increase in an external scRNA-seq dataset (GSE274819), which confirmed elevated SPP1 communication at early I/R timepoints (4 h, 12 h, 1 day) relative to Sham, across multiple ligand-receptor pairs: SPP1-CD44, SPP1-ITGAV/ITGB1, and SPP1-ITGAV/ITGB6. Decisively, spatial transcriptomics (Visium, GSE269622; 2,855 sham and 3,392 IR spots) definitively mapped this co-localization onto tissue anatomy: at 12 h, Spp1-high spots were physically adjacent to receptor-high spots far beyond chance (permutation z = +26.4, p = 0.001), whereas sham kidneys showed no spatial association (z = −2.02). Spot-level Spp1-receptor co-expression increased from r = −0.030 (Sham) to r = +0.403 (12 h), and Moran's I for Spp1 rose from 0.39 to 0.61, indicating structured, non-random patterning of the ligand only in the injured kidney (Supplementary Figure S4).

---- [79] (style=Normal) ----
The temporal and spatial convergence of the SPP1 pulse with the Fth1 iron-sequestration response at 12 h suggests a mechanistic link rather than coincidence. Osteopontin (SPP1) signals through CD44 and integrins to activate PI3K/AKT and, in turn, Nrf2 [44,45], the same transcription factor our Boolean model identified as the master regulator of the renal ferroptosis-defense program (Section 3.5). Nrf2 directly transactivates the ferritin heavy-chain gene Fth1 through an antioxidant-response element [46]. This raises the testable hypothesis that the 12 h macrophage- and thick-ascending-limb-derived SPP1 pulse is the upstream paracrine trigger engaging the Nrf2-Fth1 axis in regenerating proximal tubule, coupling the immune response to the epithelial iron-sequestration defense. Consistent with this, multi-omic studies of acute kidney injury place SPP1 signaling upstream of proximal-tubule ferroptosis regulation [47] and identify SPP1 as a marker of the injured/regenerating proximal-tubule state [48]. Whereas Hong et al. [47] established ferroptosis propagation through intercellular communication in AKI using bulk multi-omics, the present study extends this concept with single-cell temporal resolution (six timepoints), spatial transcriptomic validation (Visium), and in silico network perturbation—together delineating a specific SPP1→Nrf2→Fth1 axis rather than a general propagation signature. We emphasize that the complete SPP1-Nrf2-Fth1 pathway has been demonstrated in non-renal systems but remains correlative in the kidney; its causal role in IRI awaits direct experimental validation.

Two orthogonal computational analyses now provide initial in silico support for this hypothesis (Section 3.8). A five-method consensus (LIANA) confirmed that the 12 h SPP1 signal is reproducible across inference frameworks (Supplementary Figure S3), and independent scRNA-seq replication (GSE274819) confirmed the signaling increase across multiple ligand-receptor pairs, ruling out a single-dataset artifact (Supplementary Figure S2). Visium spatial transcriptomics (GSE269622) further demonstrated that Spp1-high and receptor-high spots are physically adjacent at 12 h (permutation z = +26.4) but not in sham, providing tissue-level anatomical validation.

(1) The scRNA-seq validation dataset (GSE274819) was analyzed only at Sham and early I/R (4 h, 12 h, 1 day reperfusion) timepoints; the later timepoints (Day 5/14/28) were not processed due to computational resource constraints. (2) The Boolean network uses binary states and cannot capture graded or probabilistic responses; future work should employ stochastic or continuous models. (3) Cell-type annotations for the discovery snRNA-seq atlas relied on original publication labels, although the independent GSE274819 scRNA-seq cohort was annotated de novo and reproduced the key SPP1 sender-receiver geometry, and Visium spatial transcriptomics (GSE269622) confirmed tissue-level Spp1-receptor co-localization at the injury timepoint. (4) All data are from mouse models; human kidney IRI may differ in cell-type composition and ferroptosis sensitivity. (5) In silico predictions require experimental validation in renal cell lines or organoid models. (6) The Boolean network is a single generic model without cell-type-specific rules; future iterations should incorporate cell-type-resolved attractor landscapes. (7) Temporal coordination was quantified from only four timepoints per gene trace; the resulting Spearman coefficients have wide confidence intervals and should be interpreted as evidence of directional consistency across cell types rather than precise correlation estimates. (8) The Boolean rules were derived from established Nrf2-centric ferroptosis biochemistry, so the identification of Nfe2l2 as the master regulator reflects the encoded network architecture; this circularity is mitigated by a model-free coupling analysis (Nfe2l2–target Spearman r = +0.74 vs Ogt −0.47; bootstrap Δr = 1.21, 95% CI [1.01–1.36], 2,000 resamples; Methods §2.8), which recovers Nfe2l2 as the driver from the data alone, so the Boolean result serves as an internal consistency check corroborated by—rather than a substitute for—independent evidence.

Building on the Visium spatial evidence presented here (Supplementary Figure S4), future studies at single-cell spatial resolution (e.g., MERFISH, Xenium) could map the coordinated temporal patterns onto precise tubular and vascular micro-anatomical zones. Experimental validation of the SPP1→CD44→Nrf2→Fth1 signaling cascade — including conditional Spp1 knockout at 12 h and pharmacological CD44 blockade — is needed to establish causality. Prospective validation of the top drug candidates (deferoxamine, imatinib) in murine IRI models would test the translational predictions of the virtual cell framework. Finally, extending this seven-step computational pipeline to human kidney transplant biopsy transcriptomic data could directly assess clinical relevance and identify patient-stratifying biomarkers.
